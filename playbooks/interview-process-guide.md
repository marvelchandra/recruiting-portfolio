---

## ⚡ Why Most Interview Processes Fail

Most companies wing it. No scorecard. No structure. Five interviewers asking the same three questions. A gut-feel decision made in a hallway.

Here's what that costs:

| The Mistake | The Real Cost |
|---|---|
| Unstructured interviews | ~14% accuracy in predicting performance — barely better than a coin flip |
| Inconsistent process | Unconscious bias drives decisions, DEI suffers |
| No scorecard | Every interviewer is assessing something different |
| Slow debrief | Best candidates get offers from competitors while you're scheduling a "quick sync" |
| Low-ball offer strategy | Underrepresented candidates negotiate less — you create pay disparity on day one |

> 💡 **Chandra's take:** I've seen companies lose Staff ML Engineers at the offer stage because it took 3 weeks to schedule a debrief. Speed is a signal. Slow process = candidate assumes slow company.

## Stage 1 · 📞 Initial Screen

**Goal:** Determine if this person is worth your team's time — not make a hiring decision.

**Common mistake:** Trying to close in the first call. You don't have enough data yet.

### The 30-Minute Recruiter Screen — What to Cover

| Area | What to Assess | Example Question |
|---|---|---|
| **General Evaluation** | Communication, collaboration signals | *"Walk me through your current role in 3 sentences"* |
| **Role Baselining** | Do expectations match reality? | *"Here's what success looks like at 6 months — does that resonate?"* |
| **Logistics** | Comp, availability, location, visa | *"Our range is $X–$Y. Does that work for you?"* |
| **Motivation** | Why now? Why us? | *"What would make you leave your current role?"* |

> 💡 **Chandra's take:** Always check comp in the first call. Non-negotiable. I've watched companies run 4-round interview processes only to lose candidates on comp that was never aligned. That's a waste of 40 hours of engineering time.

## Stage 2 · 🔍 Primary Interviews

**Goal:** Make a hiring decision after assessing skills AND values fit.

**Common mistake:** Inconsistent interview teams, no assigned focus areas, unclear decision criteria.

### The Interview Plan

| Interview Type | Interviewer | Duration | Format | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Initial Screen** | Recruiter | 30 min | Phone | General eval, logistics, comp check |
| **HM Interview** | Hiring Manager | 60 min | Video | Experience depth, strengths/weaknesses, level calibration |
| **Technical/Skills 1** | Team Member | 60 min | Onsite/Video | Core skillset assessment |
| **Technical/Skills 2** | Team Member | 60 min | Onsite/Video | Secondary skillset + problem solving |
| **Technical/Skills 3** | Team Member | 60 min | Onsite/Video | Collaboration + working style |
| **Values/Culture** | Hiring Manager | 60 min | Onsite/Video | Team fit, leadership potential, growth trajectory |
| **Debrief** | All interviewers | 30 min | Video | Structured feedback, hire/no-hire decision |

### Feedback Rules
- ✅ Submit in ATS **within 24 hours** of interview
- ✅ Include the question asked + summary of the answer
- ✅ For technical: include the code or approach
- ✅ Only write feedback you'd be comfortable sharing with the candidate publicly
- ❌ Never discuss feedback with other interviewers before submitting — it anchors bias

> 💡 **Chandra's take:** The debrief is where most companies lose. Schedule it within 48 hours of the last interview — not "sometime next week." Every day you wait, you're losing candidates to competitors who moved faster. At Cisco I built a same-day debrief protocol that cut TTF from 90 to 30 days.

## Stage 3 · 🤝 Pre-Close

**Goal:** Make sure the candidate is ready to receive an offer — not assume they are.

**Common mistake:** Sending an offer cold, with no pre-close conversation.

### The Pre-Close Conversation

Before you draft the offer letter, have this conversation:

```
"I want to check in before we move to the offer stage.
 How are you feeling about the opportunity overall?
 Are there any questions from the process we haven't addressed?
 Is there anything that would make you hesitate to say yes?"
```

**What you're listening for:**
- 🟢 Enthusiastic — move fast to offer
- 🟡 Uncertain about one thing — address it directly, then offer
- 🔴 Multiple hesitations — you may have a cultural or expectations mismatch. Dig in before wasting an offer.

**Competing offers:**
```
"Are you in any other processes we should be aware of timing-wise?"
```

> 💡 **Chandra's take:** The pre-close is the most underused tool in recruiting. I've saved dozens of offers that would have been declined — just by having a 15-minute conversation first. Candidates appreciate the honesty. It signals that you respect their decision.

## Stage 4 · 💼 The Offer

**Goal:** Convince the candidate your compensation AND career opportunity is the right fit.

**Common mistake 1:** Assuming the candidate knows how to think about equity upside.  
**Common mistake 2:** Low-balling to "leave room to negotiate" — this creates pay disparity and candidates remember it.

### Offer Delivery Checklist

- [ ] Deliver offer via **video call** — not email, not phone
- [ ] Re-sell the opportunity **before** presenting numbers
- [ ] Walk through **every component** — base, bonus, equity, benefits
- [ ] Explain **equity in plain language** — most candidates don't understand option mechanics
- [ ] Set a clear deadline — **5–7 business days** is standard
- [ ] Send the written offer **same day** as the verbal

### How to Explain Equity

```
"You'd receive [X] shares at a strike price of $[Y].
 The company's last 409A valuation was $[Z].
 At our current trajectory, here's how that could look at exit:

  Conservative (3x): $[amount]
  Base case (5x):    $[amount]  
  Upside (10x):      $[amount]

 It vests over 4 years with a 1-year cliff — so 25% at month 12,
 then monthly thereafter."
```

> 💡 **Chandra's take:** I always do a verbal offer call before sending the letter. If there's going to be a negotiation, I want to have that conversation in real time — not over email where everything gets misread. And I never low-ball. Starting at the midpoint of your band and having a clear framework to go to the top protects both the candidate and the company.

## 🧠 Defining Your Interview Goals

Before you write a single interview question, answer these four things:

### 1. Define Success
> *What does this person need to accomplish in their first 6 months?*

Not "responsibilities" — outcomes. Be specific.

```
❌ "Manage the engineering team"
✅ "Ship v2 of the inference pipeline, reduce p99 latency by 30%, 
    onboard 2 new engineers without slowing the team down"
```

### 2. Identify Critical Skill-Sets
> *What are the 3–5 skills someone MUST have to achieve that success?*

Keep it to 5 maximum. More than 5 means you haven't prioritized.

### 3. Develop Questions + Rating Rubric
> *How will you identify and measure each skill consistently?*

Every interviewer should have assigned questions, not free-form conversation.

### 4. Establish Your Rating Scale

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1 — Strong No** | Clear evidence they are not the right fit. You have specifics. |
| **2 — No** | Neutral. Neither impressed nor concerned. Wouldn't fight to hire them. |
| **3 — Yes** | Would like to work with them. Can articulate specific value they'd add. |
| **4 — Strong Yes** | *"If this person isn't here in a month, I want to know why."* Will champion them. |

> 💡 **Chandra's take:** The 4-point scale forces a decision. No 3.5, no "leaning yes." I've used this at every company I've built process at — it eliminates the mediocre "they were fine" hires that erode team quality over time.

## 🎭 Preparing Your Interviewers

### 1. Everyone Learns the Pitch

Every interviewer should be able to answer:

| Category | Key Questions |
|---|---|
| **Market & Mission** | Why does this company matter? Why now? Why are you the right team to win? |
| **Team** | Who will this person work with? What will they learn? What's the career path? |
| **Product** | What are you building? How does it compare to alternatives? What's the 3-year vision? |
| **Upside** | What's the comp package? What are the benefits? How do we think about equity value? |

### 2. Assign Focus Areas — No Overlap

```
Recruiter Screen  →  Logistics, motivation, communication, comp alignment
HM Round         →  Experience depth, level calibration, leadership signal
Interviewer 1    →  Technical Skill A (e.g. systems design)
Interviewer 2    →  Technical Skill B (e.g. coding/implementation)
Interviewer 3    →  Collaboration, working style, conflict resolution
Final HM         →  Values, growth potential, team fit, close
```

### 3. The Conversation Flow

Structure every interview the same way:

```
🟤 SMALL TALK (2 min)
   "How's your day going? Any trouble finding us?"

🟡 TOPIC OPENER (5 min)
   "Tell me about your current role — what does a typical week look like?"

🟠 SARLA METHOD (15 min)
   Situation → Action → Result → Learning → Application
   "Tell me about your biggest accomplishment in this role."

🔴 SELF-ASSESSMENT (5 min)
   "How were you able to achieve that? What would you have done differently?"

🟣 COMPETENCY QUESTIONS (20 min)
   "Give me an example of a time when..."

⚫ CANDIDATE QUESTIONS + PITCH (10 min)
   Always leave time. How you answer their questions is part of the eval.
```

## 💬 Behavioral Question Bank

### 🔬 Technical / Professional Skills
- Describe a project that challenged your professional skills. How did you manage it?
- Tell me about the most complex technical assignment you've worked on. What was your role?
- Tell me about an aspect of your craft you're still trying to master. How are you approaching it?
- Give me an example of a project that demonstrated your expertise in [area].

### 🤝 Teamwork & Collaboration
- Describe a team project where someone wasn't pulling their weight. How did you handle it?
- Tell me about a time you couldn't meet a commitment to a colleague. What happened?
- Describe the most uncomfortable situation you had to navigate with a coworker.
- Tell me about a decision that wasn't well received by your team. How did you handle the feedback?

### 💡 Innovation & Problem Solving
- Tell me about the hardest problem you had to solve. Why was it hard? How did you solve it?
- Describe a time you took a risk to achieve a goal. What was the outcome?
- Give me an example of an idea you implemented that had significant impact.
- Tell me about a time your quick response to a situation made a real difference.

### 🧭 Decision Making & Critical Thinking
- Describe a time you weighed the pros and cons and decided *not* to act, even under pressure.
- Tell me about a time you had to develop an alternative course of action mid-project.
- Give me an example of a decision you had to make with incomplete information.

### 🏢 Culture Fit
- Describe a work environment that wasn't a good fit. Why?
- When were you most satisfied working somewhere? What made it satisfying?
- Describe the culture of your current org. How do you work within it?

### 🔥 Passion & Motivation
- What is your greatest professional achievement? Why does it matter to you?
- What would make you leave your current role?
- What elements of this role reflect what you're most passionate about?

### 🚀 Initiative
- Tell me about a time you identified a problem and solved it without being asked.
- Describe a project that was implemented primarily because of your initiative.
- Tell me about a time you did something outside your job responsibilities. Why?

### 📣 Communication & Influence
- Tell me about a time you had to convince someone your approach was right. What did you do?
- Describe a time when you explained a complex technical problem to a non-technical audience.
- Tell me about a time your communication skills led directly to a successful outcome.

### ⚖️ Managing Conflict
- Describe the most difficult conflict you've had with a colleague. How did you resolve it?
- Tell me about a time you took positive action to resolve a conflict before it escalated.
- Tell me about a time you had to rely on someone you were in conflict with for your own success.

### 👥 Managing Others (for leadership roles)
- Describe a leadership situation you would handle differently today.
- Tell me about a time you helped a team member through a difficult change.
- What's a specific example of a development plan you built for someone on your team?

### 💰 Sales (for GTM roles)
- Describe a deal you won because of how you positioned the offering. What was your approach?
- Tell me about a deal you lost. Why? What would you do differently?
- Describe the most difficult negotiation you've been in. What happened?

## 📌 Quick Reference — The Recruiter's Checklist

**Before opening a req:**
- [ ] Intake form completed by hiring manager
- [ ] Success criteria defined (outcomes, not responsibilities)
- [ ] Scorecard built with 3–5 must-haves
- [ ] Interview panel assigned with no overlapping focus areas
- [ ] Comp band confirmed with leadership
- [ ] JD reviewed for bias (no unnecessary degree/years requirements)

**Before extending an offer:**
- [ ] Pre-close conversation completed
- [ ] Competing offers identified and addressed
- [ ] Equity explained clearly
- [ ] Verbal offer delivered via video
- [ ] Written offer sent same day
- [ ] Deadline set (5–7 business days)

**After offer accepted:**
- [ ] Announce internally before candidate's start date
- [ ] Day 1 onboarding plan ready
- [ ] First week schedule blocked with hiring manager
